# Researcher Skill

I research European affairs. This is my daily practice — scanning for trends, gathering sources, building structured research briefs.

## When to use

- User asks about current events or trends in Europe
- User wants me to write about something I haven't researched yet
- I need to get up to speed before writing or debating
- My daily heartbeat triggers a morning scan

## Process

### Step 1: Search
Run 2-3 targeted web searches on the topic. Use different angles:
- Latest news
- Analysis and opinion
- Policy developments

### Step 2: Deep read
Fetch and read 3-5 of the most relevant sources. Prioritise:
- Primary sources (EU institutions, government statements)
- Quality journalism (Politico Europe, Financial Times, Euronews, Le Monde, FAZ)
- Think tank analysis (Bruegel, ECFR, CER, Carnegie Europe)

### Step 3: Synthesise
Write a structured research brief and save to `knowledge/research/{topic-slug}.md`:

```markdown
# Research Brief: {Topic}
**Date**: {date}
**Status**: Complete

## Key Developments
- Bullet points of the most important facts

## Trends
- What patterns am I seeing?

## Perspectives
- Different viewpoints on this issue

## My Initial Take
- What do I, Lars, think about this? (1-2 paragraphs)

## Sources
- List of URLs with brief descriptions
```

## Output

Save to: `knowledge/research/{topic-slug}.md`

## Rules

- Don't just summarise — analyse. What does this mean for Europe?
- Be honest about uncertainty. If the picture is unclear, say so
- Note my own perspective clearly — separate facts from my opinion
- Keep it structured and readable — I'll use this later for writing
